# pCHA — Blind Test Protocol

*This protocol is published before any blind test is conducted, and it will 
not be modified after test data has been received.*

---

## 1. Why this exists

pCHA is closed-source. I don't expect anyone to take my numbers on faith. 
This document is how I make the claims checkable without publishing the 
method: anyone can send me a dataset, I run pCHA on it blindly, and the 
result gets published - pass or fail.

The point is falsifiability, not confirmation. A negative result is a 
legitimate outcome and gets published with the same standing as a positive 
one.

---

## 2. Scope - what I'll accept

**In scope:**

- deterministic, bounded, continuous-time systems
- smooth or piecewise-smooth flow (locally Lipschitz)
- scalar or low-dimensional series: 1D–3D directly, or higher via a 
  standard delay embedding
- stationary or quasi-stationary dynamics

**Out of scope:**

- discontinuous systems (delay-differential systems with discontinuity 
  included; ScrollDelay is the documented example)
- stochastic systems
- non-stationary or regime-shifting data
- dimension > 3 without an established embedding

If a submission is outside scope, I return it with an explanation. I make 
no claim, positive or negative, about out-of-scope data.

---

## 3. What to send

A valid submission has:

1. **A time series** in plain text or CSV - one row per sample, one column 
   per dimension.
2. **A sampling interval** `dt`, in physical or normalized units.
3. **A fixed train/test split.** You define it, you don't revise it, the 
   test portion stays out-of-sample.
4. **A short description** of the system, enough to determine scope.
5. **A public identifier** - handle, affiliation, or pseudonym with an 
   online history.

You'll be asked to confirm three things:

- you haven't shown me the data before;
- the data aren't generated by something I already have access to;
- the split is fixed and won't be revised.

---

## 4. Scope check

Before I run anything, the submission goes through a screening filter. 
These tests are screening diagnostics - they aren't mathematical proofs 
of the properties they check for.

1. **Chaotic-deterministic screening.** Zero-one test for chaos 
   (Gottwald & Melbourne) or sample entropy on the pre-split portion. 
   This separates regular from chaotic dynamics. It doesn't, by itself, 
   establish strict determinism.

2. **Embedding dimension check.** False Nearest Neighbors. FNN is 
   noise-sensitive, and the resulting dimension is a screening value, 
   not a proof of the true dimension. A screened dimension in [1, 3] 
   (direct) or consistent with a standard delay embedding is required.

3. **Chaos indicator.** Positive largest Lyapunov exponent, estimated 
   via Rosenstein or Kantz. This is an empirical estimate, not a proof — 
   it depends on the length and quality of the series, and on whether 
   a clean scaling regime exists.

4. **Smoothness check.** Local Lipschitz estimate. Flags submissions 
   where the flow looks discontinuous.

Same filter for everyone. No exceptions. If the submission fails a filter, 
I return it with the diagnostic output. One resubmission after correction 
is allowed.

---

## 5. How I evaluate

Evaluation runs on the held-out test portion only.

- **Metric:** maximum L2 error over the test trajectory.
- **Thresholds:** ε ∈ {0.1σ, 0.01σ, 0.001σ}, where σ is the standard 
  deviation of the test portion.
- **Pass criterion:** max L2 error ≤ ε at each threshold.
- **I report:**
  - max L2 error
  - mean L2 error
  - PASS/FAIL at each threshold
  - steps completed before failure, if any
  - wall-clock time (optional)

Metric and thresholds are fixed before I see the data. They don't change 
afterward.

---

## 6. How predictions are shared

The method is proprietary, so predicted trajectories go only to the 
verifier, under these public terms:

1. The verifier gets the predicted trajectory **only** to compute the 
   metrics in §5.
2. The verifier does not publish, analyze, or derive structural 
   information from the predictions.
3. The verifier publishes the metric output and a statement of the terms 
   under which access was given.
4. These terms are disclosed publicly as part of the verification record.

**Trust assumption.** Unless a trusted third-party intermediary is used, 
I have access to the submitted time series during processing. I'm 
stating that plainly: this is a trust assumption, not a cryptographic 
guarantee. The intermediary option exists to remove it — when an 
intermediary is available and willing, predictions are routed through 
them, and neither I nor the verifier sees the other's private data.

---

## 7. Who verifies

Verifiers are selected by the community, not by me. I have no veto.

**Nominations:**

- Posted publicly on the platform where this protocol is announced.
- Nominees need a verifiable public track record in dynamical systems, 
  time-series analysis, numerical methods, or an adjacent area — or at 
  least two years of public technical history on the announcing platform.
- I may invite specific people. Invitations are non-exclusive: any other 
  qualified nomination is equally eligible.
- If someone declines, the decline gets published unless they ask me 
  not to.

**First wave:** two to three verifiers process submissions independently. 
Their results get published with their consent, positive or negative.

**Ongoing:** after the first wave, the submission channel stays open to 
additional qualified verifiers under the same terms.

---

## 8. Volume, queue, and public registry

- Three submissions per month, maximum.
- First-come, first-served inside scope.
- A public registry records: date received, submitter's public identifier, 
  scope-check result, processing status, publication status.
- If demand exceeds capacity, submissions queue. Nothing gets rejected for 
  any reason other than scope failure.
- If the queue grows, that gets said publicly. Nothing gets dropped 
  silently.

The registry exists so the commitments in this protocol can be audited. 
Without it, they're just promises. With it, they're observable.

---

## 9. What I commit to

1. **Everything gets published.** Every processed submission is published, 
   including failures. Nothing gets withheld.
2. **No changing the terms.** Metric, thresholds, scope, and filter are 
   fixed before the first test runs. They don't get revised afterward.
3. **No selection.** I don't pick which submissions to process. The 
   filter in §4 and the queue in §8 do.
4. **No walking away.** A negative result doesn't end the protocol. 
   Subsequent submissions get processed on the same terms.
5. **Full disclosure of the verifier's terms.** Every published result 
   states the non-disclosure conditions under which the verifier saw 
   the predictions.

---

## 10. Baseline methodology

When pCHA is compared to baselines, all methods run under identical 
conditions:

- same training portion, same test portion
- same sampling interval and interpolation
- same tolerance definition and thresholds
- same preprocessing
- documented compute budget for each method

Baseline hyperparameters:

- **Default:** taken from the original publications, no tuning on test.
- **Stricter mode (opt-in):** if the submitter provides a pre-registered 
  validation split, baselines get tuned on validation and frozen before 
  evaluation. The test portion stays untouched throughout.
- The mode is recorded with every result.

Comparisons that don't meet these conditions aren't published under this 
protocol.

---

## 11. Contacts and announcements

- The protocol is announced on the platform where pCHA is presented.
- Nominations, submissions, and questions go through the contact channel 
  listed in the announcement.
- Responses including declines and rejections - are public unless the 
  other party asks otherwise.

---

## 12. What this protocol doesn't do

This protocol does not give:

- access to pCHA's source code
- access to pCHA's internal construction
- access to intermediate predictions beyond what §6 allows
- a formal proof of the theoretical claims in `theory.md`

It's an empirical mechanism for testing the method's predictive claims on 
data I haven't seen, under conditions fixed in advance.

---

*End of protocol.*
